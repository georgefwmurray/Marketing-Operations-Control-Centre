<section class="content-section">
  <div class="section-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">The opportunity</p>
    <h2>Turn a request to reduce manual work into a wider operating model.</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="prose">
    <p>This is a marketing operations and MarTech transformation case study: the goal was not simply to add AI tools, but to improve how marketing work, technology, information and accountability fit together. The model connects operational processes with the marketing technology environment, creating a more consistent way to deliver work and make decisions.</p>\n    <p>The starting point was practical: reduce repetitive work that was consuming marketing capacity. As I explored opportunities to automate individual tasks, I recognised a broader opportunity. Rather than introduce disconnected tools for isolated problems, marketing could use AI as part of a connected system — improving consistency, surfacing useful insight and giving specialists more time for higher-value work.</p>
    <p>I proposed the Marketing Operations Control Centre (MOCC), then worked with the team to shape and develop the model. The ambition was to combine operational support with better-quality information for decision-making, while keeping people accountable for judgement, approvals and customer impact.</p>
    <p>This was an opportunity I identified and developed, rather than a pre-existing transformation programme I was asked to deliver. The wider direction was to explore practical uses for AI; I shaped the case for a more joined-up approach and led the work to establish it.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="content-section">

    <p class="eyebrow">The operating model</p>
    <h2>Six layers. One connected system.</h2>
    <p>MOCC is structured as six complementary capabilities rather than a collection of disconnected automations. All six layers are live parts of the operating model. The Knowledge layer captures, maintains and makes approved organisational knowledge reusable across the team and its agents.</p>

<section class="content-section">
  <div class="section-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">Governance principles</p>
    <h2>Build trust and accountability into the model.</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="featured-project-grid">
    <article class="featured-project">
      <span class="project-category">01 / Start with value</span>
      <h3>Solve a real operating problem</h3>
      <p>Assess whether a proposed use case improves efficiency, quality or decision-making. Avoid introducing automation where existing capability already solves the problem.</p>
    </article>
    <article class="featured-project">
      <span class="project-category">02 / Make accountability clear</span>
      <h3>Keep the system understandable</h3>
      <p>Document the purpose of each capability, the decisions it supports and the people accountable for its use. Avoid knowledge becoming dependent on one individual.</p>
    </article>
    <article class="featured-project">
      <span class="project-category">03 / Keep people responsible</span>
      <h3>Human judgement where it matters</h3>
      <p>Automation can prepare, check, monitor and recommend. People remain accountable for decisions, approvals and customer-facing outcomes.</p>
    </article>
  </div>
  <div class="prose">
    <p>Governance is not an administrative layer added after implementation. It is part of the operating model from the outset: a clear business purpose, considered use of technology, transparent accountability, appropriate oversight and a commitment to keep the system useful and reliable.</p>
    <p>The model was shaped with the team, tested in practice and refined as adoption grew. The aim was to make responsible use straightforward, not create process for its own sake. This is the practical connection between MarOps and MarTech: technology is selected and governed in service of the operating model, with clear ownership and human accountability.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="content-section">
  <div class="section-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">Leadership and scale</p>
    <h2>Build capability, not dependency.</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="prose">
    <p>My role was to identify the opportunity, set the direction, develop the operating principles and lead the team through adoption. Team members contributed ideas and technical expertise, while colleagues across marketing helped identify where the model could improve day-to-day work.</p>
    <p>That collaborative approach mattered. A sustainable operating model cannot depend on one person building and maintaining everything. It needs shared understanding, clear accountability and the capability to improve the system as business needs change.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="content-section">
  <div class="section-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">What I learned</p>
    <h2>The achievement is the operating model, not the automation count.</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="prose">
    <p><strong>Start with the operating problem, not the tool.</strong> The initial aim was to reduce manual work; the wider opportunity was to improve the quality and consistency of marketing operations.</p>
    <p><strong>Governance is an enabler.</strong> Clear principles, documentation and accountability make a system easier to understand, trust and scale.</p>
    <p><strong>Design for people.</strong> Technology should release capacity and improve decisions, while specialists retain the judgement that creates good customer and commercial outcomes.</p>
    <p><strong>Build with the team.</strong> Shared ownership and capability make the operating model more resilient than a collection of solutions dependent on one individual.</p>
  </div>
</section>
