<h1>Building a marketing operations model across multiple brands</h1>
<p class="page-lead">Multi-brand marketing operations needs more than shared software. It needs a clear view of what to standardise, where brands need flexibility and who owns each decision.</p>

<h2>Make the operating model explicit</h2>
<p>I lead HubSpot operations across five brands, supporting a 25-person marketing organisation across EMEA and North America. At that scale, informal agreements stop working. Teams need clarity about ownership of platforms, data, integrations and decisions about change, with specialist teams involved where enterprise controls apply.</p>

<h2>Standardise the foundations</h2>
<p>Some things should be consistent across brands: data definitions, lifecycle stages, quality expectations and reporting. Shared standards make performance easier to compare, reduce avoidable rework and help teams move between brands without relearning core processes.</p>
<p>Standardisation should not flatten every brand into one voice. For a smaller growth brand I recommended a segmented, tested rollout in place of a blanket product promotion. It produced higher open and click-through rates than usual and the brand's highest campaign ROI that quarter. Shared standards sit underneath, and local variation sits on top.</p>

<h2>Connect marketing and sales processes</h2>
<p>CRM and automation work best when they support the whole lead journey. Agree how leads are captured, qualified, routed and measured, then use reporting to find where the process breaks.</p>
<p>Analysis showed that leads contacted on the day they enquired were much more likely to progress. A coordinated callback process and CRM integration increased the day-one contact rate from 32% to 87%, while improving PPC lead-to-sale conversion from 36% to 44%. The integration also reduced average lead-to-sales handover time by 40%.</p>

<h2>Build capability within the team</h2>
<p>A central function should make the wider team more effective, not create a queue for every small change. I supported the development of a colleague from internship into a full-time digital operations role, building technical capability and creating more ownership within the team.</p>
<p>Ideas for improvement should come from the people closest to the work. The operations team can help shape and deliver those ideas, while business stakeholders remain accountable for the outcomes they need.</p>

<h2>Measure the outcomes that matter</h2>
<p>I track response and handover time, conversion through lifecycle stages, campaign delivery quality and external spend. I keep different types of value separate: direct reductions in external spend, capacity released for other work, and wider quality or reliability improvements.</p>
<p>One operational improvement programme contributed to a 50% reduction in external content spend. I avoid publishing internal cost baselines or combining cash reductions with capacity estimates. A useful value model explains what changed, how it was measured and how confidently the result can be attributed.</p>

<h2>Make governance part of normal operations</h2>
<p>Good governance should be proportionate and practical. People need to understand who is accountable, what requires approval and where human judgement remains essential. Clear standards and ownership help new tools and processes remain dependable without creating unnecessary administration.</p>
<p>The common thread is simple: standardise what makes the system reliable, protect what makes each brand distinct, and give people clear ownership of both.</p>
