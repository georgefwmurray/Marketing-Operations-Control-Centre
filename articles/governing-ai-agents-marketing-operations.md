<h1>Governing AI agents in marketing operations</h1>
<p class="page-lead">An agent is not production-ready simply because its workflow runs. It needs a clear purpose, accountable people, appropriate controls and a way to assess whether it is still doing the right job.</p>

<h2>Start with the process, not the model</h2>
<p>The useful starting point is a repeatable business process with a clear problem. Before choosing a tool, define what the process should achieve, who owns the outcome, what information it can use and what must remain a human decision.</p>

<h2>Make accountability clear</h2>
<p>Every automation should have a business owner who understands its purpose and remains accountable for the outcome. Technical responsibility should also be clear, so that someone understands how the solution works and can support its maintenance. Ownership should not disappear when the original builder moves on.</p>
<p>Documentation should make the purpose, boundaries and current status understandable to the people who rely on the process. It should be useful, maintained and proportionate to the importance of the work.</p>

<h2>Match controls to the consequences</h2>
<p>Not every automation needs the same level of control. A tool that prepares a draft for review is different from one that changes customer data or publishes content. The potential consequences of failure should shape the testing, approval and oversight around it.</p>
<p>Controls should be practical enough to be followed consistently. That includes knowing what the system is allowed to do, where it must stop and how people can intervene if something goes wrong.</p>

<h2>Keep human judgement where it matters</h2>
<p>Human oversight should be designed into the workflow. An agent may identify inactive assets, prepare campaign materials or flag a potential issue, but a person should make decisions that affect customers, compliance or live activity where judgement and accountability are required.</p>
<p>Automation can prepare, check and route work. It should not make responsibility disappear.</p>

<h2>Review usefulness over time</h2>
<p>Platforms change, source data shifts and business rules are updated. A workflow that was useful can quietly become unreliable or unnecessary. Review whether it is still needed, whether its information remains current and whether the outcomes justify the effort required to maintain it.</p>
<p>New ideas should be assessed against business value, delivery effort, reliability, risk and overlap with capabilities already available. Not every request needs a new agent; sometimes an existing platform feature or a simpler process is the better answer.</p>

<h2>What I would tell another operations leader</h2>
<ul><li>Define the business purpose and accountable owner before building.</li><li>Set controls according to the consequences of failure.</li><li>Keep human approval for decisions that need judgement.</li><li>Review whether each automation remains useful and trustworthy.</li></ul>
<p>The aim is not to maximise the number of agents. It is to make useful automation understandable, dependable and accountable for the teams who rely on it.</p>
