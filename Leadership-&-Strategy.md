## AI Governance & Risk Philosophy

My view on scaling AI in marketing ops:

- **Build vs. buy vs. partner**: build where the workflow is proprietary to how we operate (e.g. bespoke QA logic tied to our CRM structure); buy where a mature vendor solves a generic problem cheaper than I can build and maintain it; partner where the risk or compliance surface is too specialised to own alone.
- **Governance before scale**: every agent in this system sits inside a layer (Control, Production, Data, Governance, Intelligence, Knowledge). No agent is built without a logged request, an overlap check against existing agents and tools, and approval from two Heads. No agent goes live without a defined owner, a defined failure mode and a rollback path. Once live, an Agent Health Monitor watches every run, flags failures (including silent ones on scheduled runs) to the Digital Operations team and prompts scheduled reviews to check for drift. In this system, AI governance is part of the architecture.
- **Human-in-the-loop by default**: agents are QA'd and monitored, not left unsupervised. The goal is to remove manual toil while people stay accountable.

## Operating Philosophy

The function I want to run is a marketing operations control centre where AI agents are the first line of quality, governance and insight, freeing the human team to focus on strategy, stakeholder work and the judgement calls agents shouldn't make alone. Scaling this from one team to a wider org is a repeatable pattern: define the layer, define the agent's owner and guardrails, prove it in one workflow, then replicate.

In practice, this means:

- Automating the repetitive, tedious work that consumes the team's time but has no direct line to performance or revenue, freeing capacity for the work that does. Human approval still sits at the end of every process; only the legwork is agentified.
- Replacing labour-heavy manual processes, such as campaign reviews, with agent-generated insight. Key points and findings surfaced for a human to review and act on, rather than buried in a spreadsheet or a reviewer's inbox.
- Agents sit on the front line of execution and detection; they report into the humans who remain accountable for the decisions that follow.
