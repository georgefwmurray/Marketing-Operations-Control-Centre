<section class="content-section leadership-intro">
  <div class="leadership-statement">
    <p class="eyebrow">The mission</p>
    <h2>Move marketing operations from manual execution to an intelligent, governed operating function.</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="prose">
    <p>The Marketing Operations Control Centre is one expression of that ambition: a connected system in which agents handle repeatable checks, preparation and insight, while people retain accountability for decisions, quality and customer impact.</p>
    <p>The goal is not automation for its own sake. It is to give skilled people more time for strategic work, stakeholder partnership and the judgement calls that should remain human.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="content-section">
  <div class="section-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">Operating model</p>
    <h2>Six connected capabilities</h2>
    <p>The model gives each part of marketing operations a clear role and creates a structure that can scale across brands and teams.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="layer-grid">
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="layer-index">01 / CONTROL</span><h3>Quality and compliance</h3><p>Make quality checks repeatable and visible before work reaches customers.</p></article>
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="layer-index">02 / PRODUCTION</span><h3>Content and campaign delivery</h3><p>Standardise production steps while preserving creative judgement and approval.</p></article>
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="layer-index">03 / DATA</span><h3>Reliable data operations</h3><p>Improve the consistency, readiness and flow of marketing data.</p></article>
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="layer-index">04 / INTELLIGENCE</span><h3>Performance insight</h3><p>Turn reporting into useful signals, recommendations and decisions.</p></article>
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="layer-index">05 / GOVERNANCE</span><h3>Automation oversight</h3><p>Build ownership, approval, risk and review into the system from the start.</p></article>
    <article class="layer-card layer-card--planned"><span class="layer-index">06 / KNOWLEDGE</span><h3>Documentation and memory</h3><p>Make operational knowledge reusable and less dependent on individuals.</p><span class="status-pill status-pill--planned">In planning</span></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="content-section">
  <div class="section-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">Strategic marketing partnership</p>
    <h2>Connect commercial ambition to the marketing operating plan.</h2>
    <p>Work alongside the SVP Marketing and functional leaders to shape priorities, challenge assumptions and turn business objectives into measurable plans.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="leadership-grid">
    <article class="leadership-card"><span class="outcome-number">01</span><h3>Commercial planning and performance</h3><p>Contribute to twice-yearly H1 and H2 planning with the marketing leadership team. Start with the overall marketing target, account for seasonality and year-on-year performance, then allocate monthly lead targets across functions and lead sources using historical results. Review leads, workable leads and revenue against target with senior leaders each morning, identifying where priorities or activity may need to change.</p></article>
    <article class="leadership-card"><span class="outcome-number">02</span><h3>Customer-led prioritisation</h3><p>Challenged a proposal to increase renewal communications by mapping the full customer lifecycle and identifying email oversaturation. Worked with stakeholders across functions to reduce email volume by 17%, creating capacity for more value-led communications. Click-through rates increased without negatively affecting retention.</p></article>
    <article class="leadership-card"><span class="outcome-number">03</span><h3>Investment and platform decisions</h3><p>Identified an underused A/B testing and personalisation subscription where in-house WordPress development already covered much of the functionality. Recommended cancellation to the SVP Marketing, secured approval and actioned the change, releasing budget for central marketing activity such as campaigns and PPC. Separately led competitor analysis and recommended consolidating a brand's marketing platform into the existing HubSpot environment, balancing licence costs, internal expertise and training needs, with final sign-off from the SVP.</p></article>
    <article class="leadership-card"><span class="outcome-number">04</span><h3>Constructive challenge and campaign effectiveness</h3><p>Challenge campaign audiences to ensure offers are relevant rather than defaulting to whole-database sends. For a smaller growth brand, recommended a segmented and tested rollout instead of a blanket product promotion. The approach delivered higher-than-usual open and click-through rates and the brand's highest campaign ROI of that quarter.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="content-section">
  <div class="section-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">MarTech portfolio and commercial ownership</p>
    <h2>Set the direction. Make the commercial case. Deliver the right technology.</h2>
    <p>Lead the research, evaluation and operational delivery behind the marketing technology portfolio, working with the SVP Marketing on investment decisions and with central teams where enterprise-wide controls or delivery are required.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="leadership-grid">
    <article class="leadership-card"><span class="outcome-number">01</span><h3>Technology strategy and roadmap recommendations</h3><p>Research technology opportunities, identify capability gaps and evaluate options for new platforms, integrations and improvements. Develop recommendations on what to adopt, develop, consolidate or retire, setting out the operational fit and commercial case for SVP Marketing approval.</p></article>
    <article class="leadership-card"><span class="outcome-number">02</span><h3>Vendor and commercial management</h3><p>Manage marketing technology renewals and new contract discussions, personally negotiating pricing and terms ahead of final SVP approval. Assess platform utilisation and value, challenge unnecessary spend and recommend changes where the capability or cost no longer justifies the investment.</p></article>
    <article class="leadership-card"><span class="outcome-number">03</span><h3>Budget stewardship and portfolio rationalisation</h3><p>Work within annual technology budgets agreed during Q4 planning. When new requirements emerge mid-year, first identify funding within the existing portfolio through reprioritisation, consolidation or subscription savings. Seek additional Finance funding only in exceptional circumstances.</p></article>
    <article class="leadership-card"><span class="outcome-number">04</span><h3>Integration ownership and enterprise partnership</h3><p>Own requirements, solution definition and delivery for integrations between marketing platforms. For organisation-wide integrations, represent Marketing, gather and articulate its needs, and work with the central teams leading the wider requirements and delivery. IT and Legal retain responsibility for their respective security, compliance and legal controls.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="content-section">
  <div class="section-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">Developing capability</p>
    <h2>Build capability in the team, not dependency on one person.</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="prose">
    <p>A sustainable operating model gives people ownership of the systems they help create. Team members are encouraged to identify repetitive work, propose agent ideas, refine them through review and take responsibility for continuous improvement.</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Promoted an intern to Digital Operations Executive, who now owns the continuous development of the Data Import Agent.</li>
      <li>Created a team-led approach to agent development, with ideas coming from the people closest to the workflows.</li>
      <li><strong>HubSpot AI Mentor, Agents in Action (2026):</strong> selected by HubSpot through an application process as one of a small group of mentors on its AI agents programme, while also participating as a mentee.</li>
      <li><strong>AI Mentor, Veriforce (2026):</strong> mentored two North American colleagues through end-to-end AI builds, including a sales dashboard and a social-listening agent.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="content-section">
  <div class="section-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">AI governance and risk</p>
    <h2>Governance before scale. Human accountability by design.</h2>
    <p>Automation should make the operation more dependable, not create a new category of unmanaged risk.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="principle-grid">
    <article class="principle-card"><h3>Build, buy or partner deliberately</h3><p>Build where the workflow is specific to how the organisation operates; buy where a mature product solves a generic need more efficiently; partner where specialist risk or compliance expertise is needed.</p></article>
    <article class="principle-card"><h3>Make governance part of the architecture</h3><p>Every agent request is logged and checked for overlap. New builds require approval. Live agents have an owner, a risk tier, a defined failure mode, a rollback path and a scheduled review.</p></article>
    <article class="principle-card"><h3>Keep a human accountable</h3><p>Agents can prepare, check, monitor and recommend. People remain accountable for approvals, decisions and customer-facing outcomes.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>
