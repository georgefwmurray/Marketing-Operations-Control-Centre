<section class="content-section">
  <div class="section-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">Architecture</p>
    <h2>Six layers. One connected system.</h2>
    <p>The model connects the work marketing teams need to deliver with the quality, information and governance that make delivery dependable. The Knowledge layer represents the longer-term ambition to make approved organisational knowledge reusable.</p>
  </div>
  <figure class="architecture-figure">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/images/mocc-six-layer-architecture.svg' | relative_url }}" alt="Six-layer Marketing Operations Control Centre architecture: Control, Production, Data, Intelligence, Governance and Knowledge." loading="lazy">
    <figcaption>The six-layer operating model.</figcaption>
  </figure>
  <div class="layer-grid">
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="layer-index">01 / CONTROL</span><h3>Quality assurance</h3><p>Make quality checks repeatable and visible before work reaches customers.</p></article>
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="layer-index">02 / PRODUCTION</span><h3>Content and delivery</h3><p>Support consistent production while preserving creative judgement and approval.</p></article>
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="layer-index">03 / DATA</span><h3>Data operations</h3><p>Improve the consistency, readiness and flow of marketing information.</p></article>
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="layer-index">04 / INTELLIGENCE</span><h3>Performance insight</h3><p>Turn reporting into useful signals, recommendations and decisions.</p></article>
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="layer-index">05 / GOVERNANCE</span><h3>Automation oversight</h3><p>Build accountability, oversight and continuous improvement into the system.</p></article>
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="layer-index">06 / KNOWLEDGE</span><h3>Shared organisational knowledge</h3><p>Make approved knowledge and context easier to reuse across the operating model.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="content-section">
  <div class="section-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">Governance principles</p>
    <h2>Responsible automation by design.</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="principle-grid">
    <article class="principle-card"><h3>Start with business value</h3><p>Prioritise problems where automation improves efficiency, quality or decision-making. Avoid duplication where existing tools already meet the need.</p></article>
    <article class="principle-card"><h3>Make accountability explicit</h3><p>Keep the purpose, expected outcome and accountability for each capability clear and understandable.</p></article>
    <article class="principle-card"><h3>Keep people in control</h3><p>Use automation to prepare, check, monitor and recommend, while people remain responsible for decisions and customer-facing outcomes.</p></article>
    <article class="principle-card"><h3>Maintain trust over time</h3><p>Keep documentation useful, check that the system remains fit for purpose and improve it as needs change.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>
