<section class="case-study-section">
  <h2>The business problem</h2>
  <p>GA4 data showed that the pricing page was attracting high volumes of traffic but converting a relatively small proportion of visitors into leads. The opportunity was to improve the lead-capture experience and give visitors a more compelling reason to share their details.</p>
</section>

<section class="case-study-section">
  <h2>The idea: make pricing interactive</h2>
  <p>I proposed testing an interactive pricing calculator against the existing static lead-capture form. Both versions collected the same core information: number of employees, company details and individual contact information. The difference was what the visitor got from the experience.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Control:</strong> the existing static lead form.</li>
    <li><strong>Variant:</strong> an interactive calculator that collected the same information, then requested the visitor's email address before revealing the price.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Once the price was displayed, visitors could change the employee count and see the price update. This allowed them to explore different options and understand the pricing in a more engaging, self-directed way.</p>
  <p>I originated the concept and briefed the web development team, who built the calculator. The web team carried out the A/B test analysis.</p>
</section>

<section class="case-study-section">
  <h2>Test, validate and scale</h2>
  <p>The first A/B test compared the static form with the calculator using an even traffic split. After the initial result, the approach was tested with a second brand. That test produced similar results, giving the team confidence to roll the calculator experience out across all brands.</p>
</section>

<section class="case-study-section">
  <h2>The results</h2>
  <p><strong>The interactive calculator generated 63% more completed lead submissions than the static form.</strong> Lead-to-sale conversion was lower for the calculator variant, moving from 28% to 22%. However, the higher volume of leads more than offset the lower conversion rate: overall revenue was 13% higher for the calculator variant over the same test period.</p>
  <div class="case-study-note"><strong>Commercial interpretation:</strong> The test demonstrates why lead volume should not be assessed in isolation. Although a smaller proportion of calculator-generated leads converted to sale, the variant delivered higher total revenue in the same-period comparison. The similar result in a second brand supported the decision to roll out more widely.</div>
</section>

<section class="case-study-section">

    <li>Briefed and worked with web developers, who built the calculator.</li>
    <li>Worked with the web team through the test and follow-on validation across a second brand.</li>

  </ul>
</section>

<section class="case-study-section">
  <h2>Why it mattered</h2>
  <p>This was a conversion improvement grounded in commercial performance, not just form completion. The calculator made pricing more useful and interactive for prospective customers, increased lead volume and — crucially — delivered higher revenue despite a lower lead-to-sale conversion rate. Repeating the test with another brand helped validate the idea before scaling it across the business.</p>
</section>
